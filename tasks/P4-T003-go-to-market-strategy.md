# Strategy: Go-to-Market + Target Segment Definition

**Priority:** P4 | **Type:** Management / Strategy | **Complexity:** S | **Status:** Pending

## Goal
Define who DecaShift is FOR before the first paid marketing rupee is spent.
Without a defined primary segment, every design decision is a compromise
and every marketing attempt reaches nobody specifically.

---

## The Problem (F7)
The app currently serves Grade 2–12 students, college students, and professionals
across 6+ domains. That is not a target market — it is a content catalogue.

Word-of-mouth is the only growth channel at zero marketing budget.
But there is nothing for a user to say to a friend: "you should try this,
it's perfect for [specific person in specific situation]."

---

## Segment Options to Evaluate

### Option A — Indian School Students Grade 5–10 (B2C via parents)
- Market: ~100M students in this range in India
- Pain: CBSE/ICSE exam pressure, no affordable daily practice habit
- Buyer: parent (₹199/month is a coffee for a working parent)
- Channel: WhatsApp groups, parent Facebook groups, school notice boards
- Requires: parent dashboard, progress report to share, curriculum alignment signal
- Risk: need teacher/parent as advocate, not student

### Option B — Working Professionals (B2C self-directed)
- Market: DevOps, ML, cloud engineers in India prepping for interviews/certs
- Pain: scattered prep, no daily habit, forget what they studied
- Buyer: themselves (₹199/month = trivial for employed professional)
- Channel: LinkedIn, Twitter/X tech community, dev Discord servers
- Requires: strong professional question quality (>30 per topic), real exam topics
- Advantage: self-directed, no parent/teacher intermediary, can pay directly
- Risk: smaller total addressable market, higher content quality bar

### Option C — Indian Competitive Exam Preppers (JEE, NEET, UPSC)
- Market: 2M+ JEE aspirants/year alone
- Pain: repetitive practice with habit tracking, weak area identification
- Buyer: student + parents combined
- Channel: Telegram study groups, YouTube comment sections, Quora
- Requires: curriculum-mapped questions, chapter/topic tagging, analytics on weak areas
- Risk: extremely competitive (Unacademy, PW, Allen Online)

---

## Recommendation Framework
Evaluate each option against:
1. Can we reach them without paid ads? (word-of-mouth path)
2. Do they pay without institutional approval? (sales cycle length)
3. Can we add enough content to satisfy them? (content bar)
4. Is there a "tell a friend" moment built into the use case?

---

## Deliverable
A single committed decision in `GTM.md`:
- Primary segment (one of the above or a variant)
- Why this segment first
- First 100 users acquisition plan (specific — not "social media")
- How success is measured at 100 users, 1,000 users

---

## Why P4 and Not P2
This is a strategy document, not a blocker for product development.
The app can launch to friends & family without this defined.
It becomes critical at ~100 users when paid acquisition decisions need to be made.
